# Haytham’s Shark Encyclopedia — source and editorial notes

Reviewed 22 October 2026. This is a curated introduction: nine profiles, twelve lessons and twelve glossary words, not a catalogue of every shark species. All learning copy is original. No pages, extracted passages or photographs from the supplied books are distributed with the app.

## Family-supplied reference books

- Sarah L. Fowler, *Sharks*, DK, 2017. Printed pages 4–12 (identity, size, fossils), 22–27 (mackerel, carpet and ground sharks), 28–35 (body, eggs, pups), 38–50 (food, senses, habitats, migration), 54–59 (threats and facts).
- Miranda MacQuitty / DK, *Eyewitness Shark*, 2014 edition. Printed pp. 12–13 (early sharks), 14–17 (body), 18–19 (senses), 20–23 (reproduction and pups), 24–25 (teeth and diet), 26–31 (swimming and white sharks). Rendered pages visually inspected; relevant text read in full.
- Derek Harvey, *Sharks and Other Deadly Ocean Creatures*, DK, 2016. Species profiles, especially goblin shark, printed p. 117; selected shark profiles and introductory pages read. Used for species diversity and the goblin shark explanation.
- DK, *Everything You Need to Know About Sharks*. Shark-facts spread, printed pp. 14–17 (PDF pages 16–19), used for introductory distinctions and question ideas. Avoided sensational or overly absolute claims.

## Corrections and uncertainty

- Megalodon is *Otodus megalodon*, extinct about 3.6 million years ago. Some books use older names/dates. Fossils include vertebrae, not only teeth. Exact colour, full shape and maximum length are uncertain. No fixed maximum length is taught. The dramatic illustration is explicitly artistic; Milo is fictional.
- Not every shark must swim continuously to breathe. Some pump water over their gills while resting.
- A whale shark is a fish, not a whale. Filter feeding does not mean it has no teeth.
- Avoided exact mako speed claims because reported estimates vary.
- Basking shark article on NHM contains an apparent contradictory egg-laying sentence. No basking reproductive claim is included. General reproduction lesson explains both egg laying and live birth without suggesting all sharks reproduce alike.
- Avoided old conservation categories, population estimates and species totals; old museum profiles can contain dated conservation assessments.
- Greenland longevity was checked against NOAA, but no Greenland profile or age claim is included in this edition.
- “Gentle giants” is a browsing label about feeding style, not permission to approach wildlife. Lessons recommend watching wildlife from a distance with a grown-up and following local advice.
- Kraken is marked make-believe. Cartoon helpers are distinguished from living species.

## Learning behaviour

- Profiles retain existing IDs, discoveries and progress. Nine profiles have read-aloud facts, randomized answer order, gentle retry feedback and three levels of science content.
- Profile quizzes award six pearls once per species per science stage. A retry or helper hint marks the attempt assisted. Stage advancement retains the existing requirements (five new discoveries, four independent, four distinct sharks).
- Topic quizzes award three pearls once per topic per stage and track discovery separately. They do not count toward species-based science advancement.
- Topic facts are always available; deeper text is optional at stage one and expanded at later stages. Stage three species profiles include evidence/investigation prompts.
- All text and photos are in offline cache v31. External source links need internet. Read-aloud availability offline depends on the device’s installed browser voices.
